What changes

  • Front opening: take out the old sliding glass door and inner gate. That opening (about 1.64 m) is wide enough for two 0.70 m doors side by side: one into the kitchen, one into the toilet. A short pier between them carries the partition, so very little of the cracked wall gets cut. Put one lintel across the full opening.
  • Both doors open into the main room. The toilet has its own door, and nobody walks through the kitchen to reach it.
  • Porch: remove the gate, the low wall and the grille. Push the front out so the new space is 5 ft (1.52 m) deep inside, then close it with a 9-inch block wall.
  • Kitchen on the left: 1.95 m wide, with a 1.20 m window over the sink and an L-shaped worktop.
  • Toilet on the right: 1.50 m wide, with WC, small basin, a 0.8 m shower corner and a 0.60 m high window. It's a bit smaller than the kitchen, not tiny.
  • New room window, 1.20 m wide, on the right-hand wall toward the back, away from the cracked corner.
  • Pillars at the two cracked front corners and the cracked porch-wall junction, plus new corner pillars on the front wall.

Why the kitchen is on the left: with the toilet on the right, both doors fit inside the existing opening. With the toilet on the left, you would have to cut a new door into the cracked left front corner.

The real shape (not a perfect rectangle)

The photos can't show how far the walls are out of square, so the drawing is squared up. To get the true shape, measure these with a tape and send them to me. I'll redraw it exactly.

  • Main room: all 4 wall lengths at floor level, plus both diagonals (corner to opposite corner). If the two diagonals differ, the room is out of square, and that tells me by how much.
  • Porch: the length along the room wall and along the low wall, the depth at both ends, and both diagonals.
  • Write each number on a rough sketch. Photos of the tape at each reading are fine too.

Structure, cracks and the soakaway

  • Fix the cause first. If the soakaway is wetting the ground under the front foundation, pillars alone won't stop the cracking. Locate it, stop using it, and build a new one well away from the building. Ask your engineer or planning office for the minimum distance.
  • The toilet will need its own septic tank and soakaway. Site them away from the building, not under or beside the front wall.
  • Pillars at cracks: break out the cracked section and dowel starter bars into the footing. Cast the column and tie it into a lintel band or ring beam. The engineer should set the rebar size.
  • New front section: strip foundation, DPC (the room already has rising damp), lintels over the windows and the double-door opening, and walls tied into the pillars.
  • Extend the roof and ceiling over the new section. Move the electric meter or DB off the old porch wall.
